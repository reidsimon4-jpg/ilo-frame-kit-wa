    A steel frame kit for one General Learning Area

    Two modules make one classroom. Each module here is 3.3 m wide × 12.0 m long with 2.7 m walls and a 3° roof. It is set up like the classrooms Modular by SHAPE delivers: light-gauge steel walls, an open-web steel floor cassette and a low-pitch roof, on steel bearers and block piers.

    What ILO would supply

    • Wall panels: C89 studs, tracks, noggings, headers and bracing, screwed and squared, or as numbered member kits.
    • Floor cassette trusses: open-web C89 trusses at 600 mm centres across the module.
    • Roof trusses: parallel-chord C89 trusses at 600 mm centres, raked at the roof pitch.
    • Eave blocking between trusses over the walls.
    • Shop drawings, labels and an IFC model for every module, from Infinity Framer.

    What stays with the SHAPE team

    Bearers and piers, cladding, roofing, insulation, linings, windows and doors, services, fit-out, transport and installation. These layers are drawn in grey so the frame reads clearly.

    How each member is made

    Rolled from coil on ILO's own C89 roll former. Each member comes off the machine cut to length, with its dimples, swages and service holes punched, and printed with its panel and mark (for example W8-A), so it goes together one way.

    This concept uses Infinity Framer's default settings. Production frames would be designed to SHAPE's drawings and checked by a structural engineer before anything is rolled.

    From our factory to Hazelmere

    1. Roll and assemble in ILO's factory in Bali, Indonesia.
    2. Truck to the container port in Surabaya (Tanjung Perak) and load into 40 ft high-cube containers.
    3. Sea freight to Fremantle. Sailing options and transit days to be confirmed from carrier schedules.
    4. Clear customs and road freight to SHAPE's Hazelmere factory.

    How the frames pack

    • Trusses ship assembled. Floor trusses (3.3 m) and roof trusses (3.3 m) lie flat and stack along the container.
    • Wall frames ship as numbered member kits. An assembled 2.7 m wall panel is taller than the inside of a 40 ft high-cube (about 2.35 m wide and 2.69 m high inside), so the wall members travel flat and are screwed together on SHAPE's tables, or in Perth by an ILO crew.
    • Estimate: 5 × 40 ft high-cube containers carry the frames for 30 modules (36.5 t) with the trusses assembled: 4 of trusses (9 modules' trusses a box) and 1 of wall-frame kits; 3 containers if every member ships flat (2 with nested bundles). This is our box-by-box load plan, to be confirmed with a trial load.

    What we would set up first

    One classroom as a paid pilot: frames for two modules, shipped, assembled and checked against SHAPE's drawings, with a written report on fit, tolerances and hours.

    What this model is, and is not

    • A representative module, not SHAPE's design. Size, openings and roof pitch were chosen to look like SHAPE's published classrooms, then framed in Infinity Framer.
    • Not engineered. Framing uses Infinity Framer defaults (C89-38-0.95 at 600 mm centres; default loads). A classroom floor needs a 3.0 kPa design load, so the floor trusses would likely get heavier once engineered. Wind class, tie-downs and transport and lifting loads are not checked.
    • Steel grade, thickness and coating would follow SHAPE's specification and each site's corrosion class: AS 1397:2021 G550 steel, with the coating class chosen for the site (heavier coatings near the coast).
    • Not release-checked. The concept frame has open joint and clash checks in Framer that would be closed in production design.
    • Grey layers (cladding, roofing, linings, insulation, windows, verandah, bearers and piers) are drawn only to show where the frame sits. They are not an ILO offer in this page.
    • Container count and shipping steps are estimates until confirmed with a carrier and a trial load.
